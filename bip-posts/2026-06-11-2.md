## LinkedIn
I spent an hour writing the product brief for my landing page. Then I opened the designer's handoff doc — and it contradicted my own brief on three points.

I'm building Sidekit, an always-on-top desktop companion that runs entirely on your device (dictation, a file shelf, a quick mirror before calls). I'd written a tidy messaging brief: "no account needed," show the on-device tech, lead with privacy.

The handoff said the opposite where it mattered:
— Account IS required (don't claim otherwise)
— Keep the internal architecture and model names off the page entirely
— Be honest about what's shipped vs. "coming soon" vs. "vision"

My instinct was to trust my own earlier draft. The right call was the opposite: the newer, owner-approved constraint wins. But that only works if you actually read the handoff line by line instead of skimming it because you assume you already know the product.

I almost shipped the old story. The fix took five minutes — the catch was the whole job.

When two of your own documents disagree, the newest decision is the source of truth. Reconciling them isn't busywork; it's the work.
