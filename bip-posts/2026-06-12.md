## LinkedIn
I launched my Mac app this week while too dizzy to test it. The AI ran the QA.

I'm building Sidekit — an always-on-top desktop companion for macOS: dictation that types anywhere you hold a key, a shelf to park files mid-task, a mirror before calls. Everything runs on-device.

The last 48 hours were about two unglamorous things every launch needs: knowing who your users are, and hearing from them. So Sidekit got a skippable email sign-up (no passwords, no OAuth — one field) and a feedback box with a trick I love: it's a dictation app, so you can hold the key and *speak* your bug report. The feature cost almost nothing — the box is just a text field, and dictation already types into text fields.

Then launch day arrived and I could barely sit up. Instead of postponing, Claude drove the actual app through macOS Accessibility: typed junk emails to prove validation held, walked six real launches to prove the sign-up prompt shows exactly twice and never again, turned my Wi-Fi off to confirm feedback queues offline and auto-sends on the next launch — screenshotting evidence at every step. 19 checks passed by machine. I contributed the five that needed a human voice.

Honest fine print: it's v0.1.0 and not notarized yet, so macOS will warn on first open (right-click → Open). That's the next fix.

The takeaway I keep relearning: if you write your acceptance checks precisely enough, a machine can walk them — and "I'm too sick to ship" quietly stops being a reason.

It's free: sidekit.app — and if something breaks, you can literally tell the app out loud.
